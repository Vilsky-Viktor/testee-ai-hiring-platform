# Voice interviews in 23 languages: how well Mira reads and hears each

In voice mode, Mira, testee's AI interviewer, reads each question and its options aloud, and the candidate answers by speaking: "B", "option C", "the median", or "A and D" on a question with several right answers. Voice mode works in all 23 languages testee speaks, but speech technology isn't equally good in every language. This guide shows how well it works in each, so you can choose the mode that suits your candidates.

Once the interview has started, candidates can always tap an option instead of speaking, whatever the language, and the option Mira took is shown on screen as she says it back. A question's time runs while she reads it.

## The short version

- **Very good** in English, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Hindi, Vietnamese and Chinese: answers understood at least 9 times out of 10, questions read accurately, no answer recorded wrongly in our tests.
- **Good** in Spanish, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Ukrainian, Turkish and Filipino: most answers understood; now and then Mira asks a candidate to repeat or reads a word imperfectly.
- **Fair** in Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Indonesian, Estonian and Thai: voice mode works, but candidates will tap more often. For high-stakes roles in these languages, click mode is the safer choice for now.

## Quality in each language

"Understood" is the share of spoken answers turned into the option the candidate meant. "Read accurately" is the share of questions Mira read with at most one word in ten off. "Recorded wrongly" counts answers saved as an option the candidate didn't say, the mistake that matters most; anything Mira doesn't understand, she simply asks again.

## What to know about some languages

- **Hebrew, Arabic and Persian:** Mira sometimes names the options with the letters of the language's own alphabet (alef, bet, gimel) instead of A, B, C and D as they appear on screen. The order is the same, so the first option is still the first, but candidates should answer with the letters they see.
- **Estonian:** a short "ei" (no) can sound like the letter A, so a candidate turning down a choice Mira repeats back is occasionally recorded as choosing option A.
- **Thai:** a spoken letter is sometimes heard as a Thai number ("ข้อ C" as "ข้อ สี่", option four), which can record the wrong option. Tapping is safer.
- **Japanese:** "選択肢C" (option C) was sometimes heard as another word and recorded as the wrong option; a letter on its own works better.
- **Russian and Polish:** "and" between two letters ("A и C") is sometimes heard as a third letter, and Mira asks again.

## How we measured it

For every language we wrote the same two questions, one with a single right answer and one with several, and ten spoken answers of the kinds candidates give: a letter on its own, a letter after a word ("option C"), an option read out, a position ("the first one"), two letters, two options read out, a yes and a no to a choice Mira repeats back, a change of mind, and an undecided answer that must not count. Each answer was spoken by a synthetic native-sounding voice, half of them over background noise, and heard twice, so 20 per language. Each question was read aloud eight times, 16 readings per language, and the reading compared with the text.

The numbers are a guide, not a guarantee. Twenty answers per language means a difference of a few points between two languages may be chance, and real candidates, with their own accents and rooms, differ from synthetic voices. We'll measure again when the speech models change and update this page.

## Choosing the mode

- **Voice mode** suits roles where speaking matters, candidates who prefer not to type or tap, and accessibility needs. It's best in the very good and good languages.
- **Click mode** is the default and works the same in every language. Prefer it for high-stakes roles in a language rated fair.
- Starting a voice interview needs a working microphone, so a candidate who can't use one needs click mode, set before they start.
- You can set the mode for the whole interview in its settings and change it for one candidate before they start.

In voice mode, candidates' voices are only turned into text to read their answers, never analysed in any other way, and testee never stores them. OpenAI, which turns speech into text, may keep it for up to 30 days, only to check for abuse unless the law requires longer.
